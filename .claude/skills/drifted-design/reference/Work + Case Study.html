B — Case study template · numbers-led

Frank Zoller Authentic History
Frank Zoller Authentic History · AI product

1,250 lots.
Typed by hand.

Now filled from photographs — 400+ hours a year off the client's desk.

ClientValkyrie LLC
ServiceAI product · Hosted PWA
Timeline2026, live 16 August
Outcome400+ hours saved
The challenge

Weeks of typing
before a single
item went live.

Every auction meant 1,250 lots entered by hand. Photograph, edit, renumber, then type a title, a description, a start price and an estimate range — one lot at a time, to lot 1,250.

The work was not hard. It was long, and it happened before anything could be sold, on every single auction.

What we did

Four moves,
one platform.

01

Ingestion

32,000 past items — titles, descriptions, estimates and realized prices — ingested into a hosted backend as the model's reference set.

Corpus ingestion · 1600 × 900
02

The engine

Photographs in, complete listing rows out. Repeat items pull their own history; new items get identified and described from the image.

Lister screens · 1600 × 900
03

Review triage

Confidence-scored output. Only uncertain items are flagged for a human; everything else passes straight through.

Triage queue · 1600 × 900
04

The handoff

The filled template uploads straight to LiveAuctioneers, and existing automation cross-posts to BidSpirit and the direct bidding site.

Upload template · 1600 × 900
What we found

Realized prices sat consistently below estimates across the historical set — systematic estimate inflation, invisible until 32,000 records were in one place. Nobody had asked the question because nobody could.

The numbers.

The hours figure is the client's own measurement of what the platform takes off their desk each year. Lots processed lands after the first live auction on 16 August 2026 and is not being estimated in advance.

Ehsaan · AI product, brand & launch

ClientSuleman Farooqui
ServiceAI product · Brand · Launch
Timeline2026, in production
ScopeMarketplace PWA, identity, social, launch assets
The challenge

A whole category
that only existed
in Instagram DMs.

Second-hand clothing in Pakistan moved through comment sections, WhatsApp groups and DM negotiations. There was no C2C marketplace — no listings, no search, no trust layer, nothing that looked like it was built for the people actually using it.

There is no ROAS figure on this one yet. It is pre-launch, and what exists is a marketplace, a brand and a launch system where none of the three existed before.

What we did

Three phases,
one product.

01

Twenty-three prompts to first build

React, Vite, TypeScript and Supabase, built through Lovable in twenty-three prompts, then moved to Claude Code against a nineteen-section knowledge base so the product could keep growing without losing its own rules.

Marketplace screens · 1600 × 900
02

A Gen-Z-native brand, not a marketplace template

Identity, type system and product design built for the audience that already trades this way — fast, visual, and unembarrassed about second-hand.

Identity system · 1600 × 900
03

The launch system

Social content, video and launch assets produced alongside the build, so the product and the campaign shipped as one thing rather than a handover.

Launch assets · 1600 × 900

C · Craft case
The problem it solves Not every project has a ROAS figure, and a template that assumes one makes those projects look like failures. · What changes Four things, nothing else: the hero result becomes a claim rather than a metric; the metadata bar's fourth column switches from Outcome to Scope; Results becomes What shipped — counts of artefacts, with one qualitative figure ("11 months run without us") carrying the Voltage; and the gallery grows from footnote to full section, because the work is the evidence. · What stays Identical structure, motion and type. Read both in a row and neither should feel like the lesser template.
