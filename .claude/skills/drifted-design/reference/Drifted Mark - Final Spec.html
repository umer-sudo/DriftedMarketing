The locked direction: a D whose counter has drifted off-axis, printed twice and never quite lining up. One idea stated twice — the letter is out of position inside itself, and the plate is out of position on the sheet. Everything below is measured in units of a 100-unit em square; at any output size, 1 unit = 1% of the mark's width.

Locked: 9 units, −3.5°, solid Voltage under-layer on dark; 34% tint when the mark prints in one ink on paper. Solid beats tint because a tint implies transparency and transparency implies shadow — the whole point is two opaque plates that disagree. Siren is held back for the one-per-page rare moment; outline-only reads as an unfinished state and is reserved for the motion in-between frames.

Optical corrections. Mathematical centre and visual centre are not the same object.

Overshoot 1.4. The bowl breaks the cap and baseline by 1.4 units top and bottom, or the round side reads shorter than the flat stem.

Centre shifts 1.8 left. Red is geometric centre; blue is optical, because the drifted counter loads mass to the lower right. All lockups align to blue.

Joint thinning 0.8. Where the bowl meets the stem the stroke is cut 0.8 units on both junctions so the corner doesn't blot at small sizes or in embroidery.

Three passes.

Rough. Centred counter, orthogonal offset, no rotation. Reads as a shadow; the drift has no direction.

Refined. Counter drifted onto the diagonal, plate rotated −4°, offset 7. Correct idea, offset still short of decisive and the tint muddies at 24px.

Final. Offset to 9, rotation eased to −3.5°, under-layer solid. Counter drift set parallel to the plate offset so the whole mark leans on one axis.

Step 04

Drifted

Contained (recommended). The mark drifts; the word is set clean and square. The contrast is the point — the brand can be precise and still be the thing that left. It also survives every reproduction method without a second plate.

Step 07

Clearspace & rules

Clearspace equals one stem width on all four sides, measured from the over-layer, not the under-layer — the drifted plate is allowed to eat into the margin. Nothing sets inside it, including the wordmark's own secondary line.

Step 08

Motion

The mark arrives in register and then fails to hold it. Total 1,150ms — under the 1.2s budget. The under-layer is the only thing that moves; the over-layer never shifts, so the letter is legible from frame one.
